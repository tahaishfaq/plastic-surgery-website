# Medical SEO content workflow

## Research hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Clinic-confirmed facts and the reviewing clinician for clinic-specific services, techniques, credentials, facility, pricing, and care pathways.
2. Current professional guidelines, government/regulatory sources, systematic reviews, consensus statements, and peer-reviewed research.
3. Reputable hospital or medical-association patient information for plain-language context.
4. Live SERPs, competitor pages, forums, and social discussions only to discover language, questions, fears, and content gaps—not as medical evidence.

Prefer recent sources, but retain older foundational guidance when still current. Record access date. Distinguish consensus, limited evidence, clinical practice, and clinic-specific opinion.

## Intake

Collect:

- topic and procedure;
- target market/language;
- business goal and desired conversion;
- verified service scope;
- clinician author/reviewer and credentials;
- approved clinic facts and patient materials;
- target URL or existing page;
- known Search Console/keyword data;
- publication deadline and medical-review capacity.

If no clinician can review medical guidance, create a research brief or non-clinical draft clearly marked not publishable.

## SERP and keyword research

For the live target market, record:

- dominant intent and page types;
- local pack, video, image, discussion, PAA, featured snippet, and AI-result presence;
- recurring subtopics and missing patient questions;
- freshness and authorship patterns;
- primary and secondary queries;
- exact metric source/date when volume, difficulty, CPC, or trend is stated.

Do not infer “low competition” solely from weak-looking pages. Validate using a recognized metric source plus manual SERP assessment.

### Outline

For each section specify the patient question, key answer, evidence, and internal link. Typical sections:

1. Direct answer/summary.
2. What the procedure is and is not.
3. Goals and limitations.
4. Who may or may not be a candidate.
5. Consultation and preparation.
6. Options/techniques actually offered.
7. Recovery and variability.
8. Risks, alternatives, and red flags.
9. Cost factors if relevant.
10. Questions to ask the surgeon.
11. Clinic-specific next step.

## Drafting rules

- Answer the main intent early; avoid a generic history lesson.
- Use calibrated language: “may,” “often,” “depends,” and “your surgeon will assess” where variability is real.
- Define clinical terms once and use familiar patient language afterward.
- Include concrete decision support: tradeoffs, questions, timelines as ranges, and factors that change recommendations.
- Avoid precise recovery promises. Separate common course from individual clearance.
- Avoid universal “ideal candidate” checklists that function as self-diagnosis.
- Do not tell readers to stop medication, fly, exercise, or seek/avoid surgery without clinician-reviewed context.
- Do not imply that cosmetic surgery treats self-esteem or guarantees confidence.
- Do not use patient stories or outcomes without documented permission and review.
- Do not add a keyword merely to increase density.

## Citation and attribution

- Cite the source supporting each material medical claim near the claim.
- Link to the original guideline, paper, regulator, or association—not a search-results page.
- Do not copy source language. Quote only when necessary and keep quotations short.
- Display a source list, author/reviewer identity, publish date, modified/reviewed date, and correction contact.
- Ensure Article/BlogPosting data matches visible authorship and dates exactly.

## Conversion copy

Use a low-pressure CTA appropriate to the page stage:

- Awareness: “Read how consultation and candidacy are assessed.”
- Consideration: “Prepare your questions for a private consultation.”
- Decision: “Request a consultation to discuss suitability, risks, options, and a personalized quote.”

Never use countdowns, limited slots, shame, fear, guaranteed results, or “book now before it is too late.”

## Medical/compliance review checklist

- [ ] Service and technique are actually offered.
- [ ] Doctor identity, role, qualifications, affiliations, and registration are verified.
- [ ] No unsupported superlative, comparison, count, success rate, safety promise, or outcome promise remains.
- [ ] Benefits are balanced with risks, limits, alternatives, and variability.
- [ ] Medication, anesthesia, recovery, travel, pregnancy, and complication advice has clinician review.
- [ ] Prices and inclusions are approved and dated, or only cost factors are described.
- [ ] Patient media/testimonials have documented consent and accurate source attribution.
- [ ] Medical citations are authoritative and current.
- [ ] Author, reviewer, publish date, review date, and disclaimer are visible.
- [ ] The page does not diagnose or replace individualized care.

## SEO/editorial QA checklist

- [ ] One distinct intent and canonical target.
- [ ] No conflict with an existing procedure page or article.
- [ ] Useful title, metadata, H1, headings, and direct opening.
- [ ] Original value beyond summarizing competitors.
- [ ] Natural terminology and no keyword stuffing.
- [ ] Descriptive internal anchors and relevant related content.
- [ ] Original/approved imagery with useful alt text.
- [ ] Structured data mirrors visible content.
- [ ] Mobile readability and conversion path checked.
- [ ] Index only after final clinic and medical approval.

## Refresh workflow

At least annually for medical evergreen content—and sooner after guideline, service, clinician, price, or safety changes—recheck sources, SERPs, internal links, query performance, conversion quality, schema, author/reviewer details, and content overlap. Show a truthful modified date only when a meaningful review or update occurred.
