If you searched for the "best plastic surgeons in Islamabad" or the "best plastic surgeons in Pakistan," you are probably looking for a surgeon who is appropriately qualified, experienced in the procedure you are considering, transparent about risks, and available throughout recovery.

There is no single objective ranking that can identify the right surgeon for every patient or every procedure. A surgeon may have extensive experience in one area of plastic surgery but not another, and a safe recommendation must account for your health, goals, procedure, facility, anaesthesia plan, and follow-up needs.

The most useful way to find the best fit is to compare surgeons using evidence you can verify.

## Why a "best plastic surgeons" list can be misleading

Online directories, social-media popularity, testimonials, and clinic-created rankings can help you discover names, but they do not independently prove a surgeon's registration, training, current competence, or suitability for your operation. Paid placement may also affect which providers appear most prominently.

Patient reviews can describe communication and service experiences, but they rarely provide enough clinical information to compare outcomes fairly. Before-and-after photographs also show selected cases, not the full range of possible results or complications.

Treat every shortlist as a starting point. Verify each surgeon yourself, meet the person who would operate on you, and compare the complete care plan rather than choosing from a headline claim.

## 1. Verify PM&DC registration and registered qualifications

Start with the official [Pakistan Medical and Dental Council practitioner search](https://www.pmdc.pk/Home?keyWord=registration). The register allows patients to search by a practitioner's name or registration number and view licence validity and registered qualifications.

Check that:

- the full name matches the surgeon you plan to consult;
- the registration is current;
- the postgraduate qualification described by the clinic appears in the official record;
- the clinic explains any difference between the advertised title and the registered qualification.

Ask the clinic for the surgeon's PM&DC registration number if it is not clearly displayed. Registration confirms regulatory status and recorded qualifications; it does not, by itself, establish experience with a specific procedure or guarantee an outcome.

Membership in a relevant professional association can be an additional check. The [Pakistan Association of Plastic Surgeons member directory](https://paps.org.pk/?p=8138) may help with that step, but professional membership should not replace PM&DC verification.

## 2. Match the surgeon's experience to your procedure

Plastic surgery covers reconstructive and aesthetic procedures across different parts of the body. Ask about recent, procedure-specific experience rather than accepting a general statement such as "years of experience."

Useful questions include:

- How often do you perform this exact procedure?
- Which patients are not suitable for it?
- What alternatives should I consider?
- What are the common and serious risks in my situation?
- How do you manage complications or an unsatisfactory result?
- Who would perform a revision if one became medically appropriate?

A responsible surgeon should explain both the potential benefit and the limitations. The answer may sometimes be that surgery is not advisable, another treatment is more appropriate, or further medical assessment is needed.

## 3. Meet the surgeon who would perform the operation

Do not make a surgical decision after speaking only with a salesperson, coordinator, or social-media representative. The surgeon who would operate should assess your medical history, medications, previous procedures, expectations, and relevant physical findings.

The UK's [NHS guidance on choosing a cosmetic-procedure provider](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/cosmetic-procedures/advice/choosing-who-will-do-your-procedure/) recommends meeting the person who will perform the procedure and asking about qualifications, experience, complications, aftercare, and what happens if something goes wrong. Although UK registration rules do not apply in Pakistan, those decision-making questions are useful in any country.

You should have enough time to ask questions and consider the answers. Be cautious if a clinic pressures you to pay immediately, promises a guaranteed result, or discourages a second opinion.

## 4. Ask where surgery will take place and who will provide anaesthesia

The surgeon is only one part of surgical safety. The International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery describes safe care as a system involving the appropriate patient, procedure, surgeon, and facility, with suitable anaesthesia and monitoring. Read the [ISAPS patient-safety framework](https://www.isaps.org/discover/about-isaps/what-we-do/how-isaps-promotes-safety/).

Before agreeing to surgery, ask:

- What is the full name and location of the operating facility?
- What level of surgery is the facility equipped to provide?
- Who will administer and monitor anaesthesia, and what are their qualifications?
- What monitoring and recovery support will be used?
- How does the team respond to an emergency?
- Is there a documented transfer plan if hospital-level care is required?

The answers should be specific to your planned procedure and health needs. A polished consultation room or social-media page does not establish the safety of an operating facility.

## 5. Evaluate consent, communication, and privacy

Informed consent is more than signing a form. Before deciding, you should receive a clear explanation of the proposed operation, expected recovery, meaningful risks, limitations, alternatives, and the option of no treatment.

Good communication includes realistic language. No ethical clinician can guarantee an exact appearance, a scar-free result, or freedom from complications. You should also be able to ask who will take clinical photographs, how those images will be stored, and whether separate permission is required before they can be used for education or marketing.

If privacy, a chaperone, or access to female clinical staff matters to you, discuss the preference when booking. Women considering a female surgeon can also read our guide to [choosing a female plastic surgeon in Islamabad](/news/female-plastic-surgeon-islamabad).

## 6. Understand the follow-up and complication plan

Surgery does not end when you leave the operating facility. Ask for a written follow-up plan that explains:

- when routine reviews will take place;
- which clinician will see you at each stage;
- whom to contact after hours;
- which symptoms require urgent assessment;
- how complications are investigated and treated;
- what the clinic's revision policy covers and excludes;
- which additional costs may apply.

If you live outside Islamabad or outside Pakistan, discuss how long you should remain nearby, whether travel plans may need to change, and who will coordinate care after you return home. Our [international patient information](/international-patients) can help you prepare questions, but your surgeon must provide advice for your individual situation.

## 7. Review photographs and testimonials carefully

Before-and-after photographs may help you discuss scar position, proportions, and the range of possible changes, but they cannot predict your result.

When a clinic shows photographs, ask whether they:

- show the same procedure you are considering;
- belong to that surgeon's own patients and are used with consent;
- use consistent lighting, positioning, and timing;
- include outcomes that reflect different starting points;
- explain how long after surgery the final image was taken.

Online testimonials should supplement, not replace, checks of registration, qualifications, procedure-specific experience, facility arrangements, consent, and follow-up.

## 8. Compare the total care cost, not only the headline fee

The lowest advertised price is not automatically the safest or most economical option. Ask for an itemised written quotation and check whether it includes:

- the surgeon's fee;
- operating-facility charges;
- anaesthesia fees;
- pre-operative tests;
- medicines and surgical garments;
- scheduled follow-up visits;
- treatment of complications;
- revision surgery, if clinically appropriate;
- taxes or other charges.

Do not assume that every clinic includes the same services. A clear quotation helps you compare like with like and plan for costs that may arise during recovery.

## Plastic surgeon consultation checklist

Take this checklist to each consultation:

1. What is your PM&DC registration number, and which qualifications are registered?
2. How much recent experience do you have with this exact procedure?
3. Why might I be suitable or unsuitable?
4. What alternatives, including no surgery, should I consider?
5. What are the common, serious, and patient-specific risks?
6. Where will surgery take place?
7. Who will provide anaesthesia and monitor recovery?
8. Who will perform each part of the operation?
9. What result is realistic, and what cannot be promised?
10. What will recovery involve, including time away from work and travel?
11. Who will provide follow-up and urgent support?
12. What is included in the total fee?
13. How are complications and revisions handled?
14. Can I take time to decide or seek a second opinion?

## Warning signs when comparing surgeons

Pause and investigate further if you encounter:

- a registration number or qualification that cannot be verified;
- guaranteed results or claims that an operation is risk-free;
- pressure to book because a discount expires immediately;
- consultation only with sales staff before payment;
- refusal to identify the operating facility or anaesthesia provider;
- vague answers about complications, emergency care, or follow-up;
- copied, misleading, or unexplained before-and-after photographs;
- advice to hide important medical information or ignore another clinician's concerns.

## Choosing in Islamabad versus elsewhere in Pakistan

The same verification principles apply whether you are comparing surgeons in Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Lahore, Karachi, Peshawar, or another city. Location still matters because safe care may require in-person assessment, access to the operating team, and multiple follow-up visits.

Consider how quickly you could return if a concern develops. Travel convenience should not replace qualification and safety checks, but a realistic aftercare plan is part of choosing the right provider.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is the best plastic surgeon in Islamabad or Pakistan?

There is no reliable universal answer. The best fit for an individual patient depends on verified registration and qualifications, relevant experience, the planned procedure, facility and anaesthesia arrangements, communication, and continuity of follow-up. Use those criteria to create a shortlist rather than relying on a ranking.

### How can I verify a plastic surgeon in Pakistan?

Search the official [PM&DC practitioner register](https://www.pmdc.pk/Home?keyWord=registration) using the surgeon's name or registration number. Check licence validity and registered qualifications, then ask the surgeon about recent experience with your specific procedure.

### Does PM&DC registration prove that a surgeon is experienced?

No. It helps verify regulatory status and registered qualifications. You must separately assess recent procedure-specific experience, facility arrangements, consent, outcomes discussion, and follow-up.

### Should I choose a surgeon based on reviews?

Not on reviews alone. Reviews may help you understand communication or service experiences, but they do not replace professional verification or an individual medical consultation.

### Is a "cosmetic surgeon" always a plastic surgeon?

Titles can be used inconsistently in advertising. Ask for the clinician's full name and registration number, check their qualifications in the PM&DC register, and ask what training and recent experience they have for the procedure you are considering.
