Patients travel to Pakistan for plastic surgery for many reasons, including family ties, cost planning, access to a preferred surgeon, or the ability to recover with local support. Travel adds logistics to an already important medical decision. A useful plan covers consultation format, records, length of stay, accommodation, companion support, emergency contacts, and follow-up continuity.

This guide outlines practical questions for international patients considering Rahman Plastic Surgery in Islamabad. It does not replace individualized medical advice about when you may fly, return to work, or travel after an operation.

## Start with consultation clarity

Before booking flights for surgery, confirm what stage you are planning for:

- an initial information call;
- a virtual pre-consultation;
- an in-person consultation only;
- consultation plus surgery in one trip;
- surgery after a separate earlier visit.

Some decisions require in-person examination, imaging, or laboratory tests. Others may begin remotely with records review and then continue in clinic. Ask the team which format applies to your procedure and health history.

Review our [international patients page](/international-patients) and [request a consultation](/contact#consultation) when you are ready to discuss your goals.

## Records and photographs to prepare

Organised information helps the surgical team assess suitability before you travel.

Useful items may include:

- a concise medical history and medication list;
- allergy information;
- previous surgery reports;
- relevant laboratory or imaging results;
- photographs if the clinic requests them for planning;
- a list of questions about risks, recovery, and follow-up.

Do not withhold important health information because you worry it may affect eligibility. Accurate history supports safer planning.

If privacy matters, ask how records and images are stored, who can access them, and whether separate consent is required for educational or marketing use.

## Choosing the right companion and support plan

Many patients benefit from a responsible adult who can help with transport, medication reminders, meals, and early post-operative errands. Discuss:

- whether the clinic requires a companion for certain procedures or anaesthesia plans;
- whether your companion needs to stay overnight near the facility;
- how communication will work if your companion does not speak the same language as the clinical team;
- what tasks you may not be able to do independently in the first days after surgery.

Support needs vary by procedure. Abdominoplasty, combined body-contouring surgery, and larger-volume liposuction often require more assistance than minor procedures.

## Length of stay: build in flexibility

Advertised surgery dates are only one part of the itinerary. You may also need time for:

- pre-operative consultation and tests;
- scheduling changes due to medical findings;
- early recovery and dressing changes;
- post-operative reviews;
- unexpected swelling or minor concerns that require in-person assessment.

Ask the clinic for a provisional timeline, then keep buffer days rather than booking the earliest possible return flight before surgeon clearance. **Only your treating team can advise when flying is appropriate for you.**

## Accommodation considerations

Choose lodging that supports rest and easy clinic access. Practical factors include:

- proximity to the clinic and emergency services;
- elevator access if stair climbing is limited early in recovery;
- quiet environment and comfortable sleeping position;
- refrigerator access for food and medications;
- hygiene and privacy for garment and dressing care;
- reliable mobile connectivity for follow-up communication.

Luxury amenities are less important than safety, rest, and the ability to reach your surgical team if needed.

## Travel before surgery

For pre-operative travel, consider:

- arriving rested before consultation or surgery;
- carrying medicines in original containers with labels;
- keeping clinic contact details and appointment confirmations accessible;
- allowing time to adjust after long flights before major medical appointments if possible;
- confirming payment, quotation, and cancellation policies in writing.

Avoid making non-refundable travel arrangements that assume surgery will definitely proceed before medical clearance is complete.

## Travel after surgery

Post-operative flight timing depends on the procedure, healing progress, clot-prevention measures, and individual risk. General internet advice is not a substitute for surgeon guidance.

Questions to ask include:

- how many days or weeks you should remain in Islamabad before flying;
- whether compression garments should be worn during travel;
- how to walk and hydrate during long journeys if approved to travel;
- whether blood-thinning medicines or injections may be recommended for your situation;
- what symptoms during travel require urgent care;
- whether a fit-to-fly or medical letter is needed.

Do not hide travel plans from your surgeon to keep an earlier flight. A delayed departure is often safer than flying before clearance.

## Communication and follow-up after you return home

Continuity matters after you leave Pakistan. Clarify:

- who is the primary contact for routine questions;
- how after-hours concerns are handled;
- whether follow-up can occur by video for selected visits;
- which symptoms require local urgent assessment in your home country;
- whether the clinic can provide records for your local doctor if needed;
- how long the team remains available for post-operative questions.

Keep copies of operative notes, discharge instructions, and medication lists accessible when you travel home.

## Financial and administrative planning

International care planning should include more than the headline surgical fee. Confirm whether your quotation may include or exclude:

- surgeon fee;
- facility and anaesthesia charges;
- pre-operative tests;
- garments, medicines, and dressings;
- follow-up visits;
- complication management within defined limits;
- revision policy terms;
- taxes or administrative fees.

Ask about payment methods, currency, refunds if surgery is deferred for medical reasons, and whether travel insurance covers planned elective surgery. Many standard policies exclude cosmetic procedures unless specifically arranged.

## Warning signs in international care planning

Pause if a clinic:

- promises immediate surgery without adequate assessment;
- guarantees results or downplays complications;
- refuses to identify the operating surgeon or facility;
- gives fixed flight timelines before examining you;
- offers no clear after-hours contact plan;
- pressures you to transfer funds before basic verification is complete.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I have my first consultation online from abroad?

Many clinics offer remote initial discussions, but in-person assessment is often required before final surgical planning. Ask Rahman Plastic Surgery what is possible for your procedure.

### How long should I stay in Islamabad after surgery?

It depends on the operation, healing, and your personal risk profile. Your surgeon should provide individualized guidance rather than a one-size-fits-all number.

### Can a family member in Pakistan support my recovery?

Local support can be valuable. Tell the clinic who will assist you so instructions can be shared appropriately, while respecting your privacy preferences.

### Should I tell my doctor at home about my surgery plans?

If you have ongoing medical conditions or regular prescriptions, involving your usual doctor may help with pre-operative optimisation and post-operative continuity. Ask the surgical team what information would be helpful.

### Is medical tourism safe?

Safety depends on the same factors as local care: appropriate patient selection, qualified clinicians, suitable facility and anaesthesia care, informed consent, and reliable follow-up. Travel adds logistics but does not reduce the need for medical verification.
